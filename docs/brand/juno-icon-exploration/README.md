# JUNO — Synastry Guide · App Icon Exploration

Six standalone icon directions for **JUNO**, a premium synastry-led relationship
discovery app. JUNO helps people explore romantic connection through birth-chart
context and synastry insight — **no swipe mechanics, no Tinder-style matching**.
The brand voice is mature, romantic, intentional, premium. The name references
the Roman goddess Juno (partnership, commitment, relationships).

This folder is **exploration only**. Nothing here is a production icon and
nothing outside this folder was modified.

## Shared system

- All icons are `1024×1024` and render on a rounded-square app-icon tile
  (`rx="224"`, iOS-style corner radius).
- Palette (only): near-black `#08090b`, deep ink `#11141b`, warm gold `#e9c873`,
  soft blue `#7aa7ff`, off-white `#e7e9ee`.
- No cliché astrology purple. No heart, no couple figures, no match arrow, no
  swipe cards, no crystal ball, no AI sparkle.
- Pure vector — all letterforms drawn as stroked paths, no embedded raster, no
  external fonts in the icon files (the contact sheet uses system fonts for
  caption text only).

## Concept A — Juno Glyph / Crown

**Description.** A vertical staff bearing a gold ring with a soft-blue core,
grounded on a horizon bar, topped by three restrained rising points. It
abstracts the astronomical Juno glyph (the asteroid 3 Juno is drawn as a
star/sceptre on a staff) into a partnership emblem.

**What it communicates.** Commitment, devotion, a relationship that is held up
and grounded. The ring reads as union; the horizon bar reads as a shared
foundation; the three points read as aspiration rather than royalty.

**Risk.** The three points can still skim a "crown" reading at a glance, which
risks a generic premium/royalty cue. The composition is also the most literal
and busiest of the six.

- **App Store fit:** 7/10
- **Play Store fit:** 7/10
- **Small-size legibility:** 6/10 — the crown points and horizon dots are the
  first details to muddy at 29×29.
- **Recommendation:** **No** — strong story, but the crown cue is a liability
  and the small-size read is the weakest in the set.

## Concept B — Twin Orbits

**Description.** Two tilted elliptical orbits — one gold, one soft blue — that
cross, with a defined shared centre and a small body riding each orbit.

**What it communicates.** Synastry at its most direct: two charts, two paths,
in relation to one another. The crossing is the relationship; the shared centre
is the connection point. Reads as astronomy and partnership without any dating
cliché.

**Risk.** Two crossing ellipses are a fairly common "orbit/atom" motif, so it
needs the asymmetric tilt and the two colours to feel ownable. At very small
sizes the two ellipses can fuse into a single blob.

- **App Store fit:** 8/10
- **Play Store fit:** 8/10
- **Small-size legibility:** 7/10 — the centre node survives; the riding bodies
  are the first to drop out.
- **Recommendation:** **Yes** — clear, on-concept, and a strong fallback.

## Concept C — Chart Union

**Description.** Two abstract birth-chart wheels (rings with house spokes) that
overlap, with white aspect lines drawn across the shared lens and a clear union
node at centre.

**What it communicates.** The most explicit synastry statement in the set —
this is literally two natal charts brought into relationship. It signals depth,
craft, and a serious astrology product.

**Risk.** It is the most detailed concept. The house spokes and aspect lines
are fine-grained and will not survive the smallest icon sizes — it risks
collapsing into a noisy double-ring at 29×29.

- **App Store fit:** 8/10
- **Play Store fit:** 7/10
- **Small-size legibility:** 5/10 — detail-dense; the spokes and aspect lines
  disappear small.
- **Recommendation:** **No** — excellent for marketing/illustration, too fine
  for an app icon.

## Concept D — Guided Connection

**Description.** Two grounded points (gold and soft blue) joined by a single,
calm sweeping arc, with a considered waypoint at the apex and a faint orbital
field for context. The arc gradient transitions gold → blue.

**What it communicates.** An intentional, deliberate path between two people —
connection as a journey that is guided, not gamified. The waypoint reads as
"meeting point, chosen." It deliberately avoids the directional arrow of dating
apps; the arc is a complete, symmetrical curve.

**Risk.** A point-arc-point composition is a recognisable connectivity motif
(messaging, travel, network apps), so it leans slightly generic and is the
least overtly astrological of the six.

- **App Store fit:** 8/10
- **Play Store fit:** 8/10
- **Small-size legibility:** 8/10 — three bold shapes and one thick arc; reads
  cleanly down to 29×29.
- **Recommendation:** **Yes** — calm, premium, very legible; slightly generic.

## Concept E — Dark Premium Tile

**Description.** A dark rounded tile with a soft radial depth, a faint inner
hairline, and a warm gold edge. At centre, two interlocking gold rings of
synastry with a soft-blue star at the shared point and a subtle blue ambient
glow behind the emblem.

**What it communicates.** Premium and intentional first, synastry second. The
interlocked rings are an unmistakable, mature symbol of union and commitment
(the Juno theme), and the star marks the point of connection. The depth is
restrained — ambient, not glossy.

**Risk.** Two interlocking rings can read as wedding bands, which is a slightly
narrower (marriage) signal than "relationship discovery." The gold edge must
stay subtle or it tips toward cheap-luxe.

- **App Store fit:** 9/10
- **Play Store fit:** 9/10
- **Small-size legibility:** 9/10 — two bold rings plus one star; the strongest
  small-size read in the set, and the dark tile makes it pop on any home screen.
- **Recommendation:** **Yes** — the strongest all-round candidate.

## Concept F — Mythic Minimal J

**Description.** A discreet stylized "J" drawn as one continuous calligraphic
stroke, its foot terminating in a small gold planet sitting on a soft-blue
orbit ring, with a single counter-body riding the orbit opposite.

**What it communicates.** Brand-forward and mythic-minimal. The orbit ties the
letterform to astronomy and to the idea of two bodies in relation; the planet
on the ring keeps it from being a plain monogram.

**Risk.** It is still fundamentally a single-letter mark, so it carries the
least conceptual meaning about synastry/relationships until the brand is known.
The serif J also needs careful optical balance not to look like a generic
lettermark.

- **App Store fit:** 7/10
- **Play Store fit:** 7/10
- **Small-size legibility:** 8/10 — the J and orbit hold up; the counter-body
  drops out small.
- **Recommendation:** **No** — handsome and ownable, but weaker as a primary
  icon; better reserved as a secondary/wordmark glyph.

## Overall recommendation — ship **Concept E — Dark Premium Tile**

Concept E is the recommended icon to ship. It is the best balance of every
requirement at once:

- **Premium and mature by construction** — the dark tile, restrained gold edge,
  and ambient (not glossy) depth deliver the premium feel without any cliché.
- **On-concept** — interlocked rings are a clear, dignified symbol of
  partnership and commitment, directly serving both the Juno namesake and the
  synastry-led product; the soft-blue star marks the point of connection.
- **Best small-size legibility (9/10)** — two bold rings and one star read
  cleanly from 1024×1024 down to 29×29, where the more detailed concepts (C in
  particular) collapse.
- **Strong on both stores** — the dark tile stands out against the typically
  light app-grid backgrounds of both the App Store and Play Store.

If a more explicitly astronomical mark is preferred over the union rings,
**Concept B — Twin Orbits** is the recommended runner-up: it states "two charts
in synastry" most directly while keeping a clean, legible silhouette.
